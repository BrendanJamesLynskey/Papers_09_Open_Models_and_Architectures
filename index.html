The Five Publications

Five papers chart the arc of open-weights large language models — from the first research checkpoint that proved you could beat GPT-3 with a fraction of the parameters, to a frontier-class 671B mixture-of-experts trained for a famously small budget. Each one introduced an architectural or efficiency idea that the whole field now takes for granted.

01

LLaMA — Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models

Touvron et al. (Meta AI) · 2023 · arXiv:2302.13971

The problem

The prevailing wisdom (and the GPT-3 paper) suggested that capability scaled with parameter count, so the largest models won. But Hoffmann et al.'s Chinchilla work showed that, for a fixed compute budget, most large models were badly under-trained. Worse, the strongest models were closed APIs trained on undisclosed data, leaving the research community unable to study, reproduce, or build on them.

The contribution

LLaMA is a family of decoder-only transformers (7B, 13B, 33B, 65B) trained only on publicly available data — no proprietary or instruction-tuning corpora. By spending the compute budget on more tokens rather than more parameters, LLaMA-13B outperforms GPT-3 (175B) on most benchmarks while being small enough to run on a single GPU. The architecture bundles three now-standard refinements over the vanilla transformer:

Pre-normalisation (RMSNorm)

Normalise the input of each sub-layer instead of the output, using RMSNorm (Zhang & Sennrich) rather than LayerNorm. Drops the mean-centring and bias terms — cheaper and more stable for deep stacks.

SwiGLU activations

Replace the ReLU feed-forward with a SwiGLU gated unit (Shazeer). A gating branch multiplies a SiLU-activated projection, improving quality per parameter; the hidden dim is scaled to keep FLOPs comparable.

Rotary embeddings (RoPE)

Drop absolute position embeddings for RoPE (Su et al.), which rotates query/key vectors by a position-dependent angle. Encodes relative position directly in attention and extrapolates more gracefully.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

LLaMA's weights leaked within a week and, despite a research-only license, catalysed the entire open-weights ecosystem — Alpaca, Vicuna, llama.cpp, QLoRA and the whole local-inference stack descend from this checkpoint. The architectural triad (RMSNorm + SwiGLU + RoPE) became the de-facto template that almost every later open model, including the four that follow, builds on.

The takeaway

Tokens, not just parameters, buy capability. A well-trained 13B model on consumer hardware can match a 175B API model — which is why "small, open, fine-tunable" became a viable engineering strategy overnight.

02

Llama 2 — Open Foundation and Fine-Tuned Chat Models

Touvron et al. (Meta AI) · 2023 · arXiv:2307.09288

The problem

LLaMA was research-only and base-only: no commercial license and no aligned chat model. To make open weights genuinely useful in products, you need (1) a license businesses can ship on, and (2) a model that is helpful and safe in multi-turn conversation, not just a raw next-token predictor.

The contribution

Llama 2 ships 7B/13B/70B base models under a permissive commercial license, plus Llama 2-Chat, aligned with a full RLHF pipeline. The alignment recipe and two attention refinements are the headline ideas:

  • SFT → RLHF: supervised fine-tuning followed by iterative RLHF, combining rejection sampling (sample many, keep the best per a reward model) with PPO; two separate reward models score helpfulness and safety independently.
  • Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) at 70B: multiple query heads share a single key/value head, shrinking the KV cache and speeding up inference at long context with negligible quality loss.
  • Ghost Attention (GAtt): a fine-tuning trick that keeps a system instruction (e.g. "always answer in French") salient across many turns, fixing the classic "model forgets the system prompt" failure.
  • Extensive safety & red-team work: safety-specific RLHF, context distillation, and a documented red-teaming programme.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

Llama 2 is the model that made open weights shippable. The permissive license unblocked commercial deployment; GQA is now standard in nearly every modern model because it is the single cheapest way to cut KV-cache memory; and the paper is still the most detailed public description of an end-to-end RLHF pipeline you can read.

The takeaway

Alignment is an engineering pipeline, not a single step. If you ever fine-tune a chat model, the SFT → reward-model → RLHF structure here is the canonical reference — and GQA is free latency you should expect from any serving stack.

03

Mistral 7B — Sliding-Window Attention & GQA

Jiang et al. (Mistral AI) · 2023 · arXiv:2310.06825

The problem

Standard self-attention costs O(n²) in sequence length, so long context is expensive in both compute and KV-cache memory. Llama 2 13B set the small-model quality bar — could a 7B model beat it while being cheaper to serve at long context?

The contribution

Mistral 7B outperforms Llama 2 13B across benchmarks while using fewer parameters, by combining three efficiency mechanisms under an Apache 2.0 license:

  • Sliding Window Attention (SWA): each token attends only to the previous W tokens (W = 4096). Because attention stacks, information still propagates across layers — after k layers a token can reach roughly k×W tokens back — giving long effective context at linear cost.
  • Grouped-Query Attention (GQA): shared KV heads for faster inference and a smaller cache.
  • Rolling-buffer KV cache: since attention never looks back further than W, the cache is a fixed-size ring buffer of length W — memory stops growing with sequence length.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

Mistral 7B became the default "small, strong, truly-open" base for fine-tuning and local deployment, and proved that architecture, not just scale, drives the quality/cost frontier. The rolling-buffer cache is a clean illustration of how a serving constraint (bounded memory) can be designed directly into the attention pattern.

The takeaway

When context length hurts your memory budget, the fix is often architectural, not bigger hardware. SWA + a rolling KV cache give you long effective context with a flat memory footprint — and Apache 2.0 means no license friction.

Mixtral of Experts — Sparse Mixture-of-Experts

Jiang et al. (Mistral AI) · 2024 · arXiv:2401.04088

The problem

Quality scales with parameters, but a dense model pays for every parameter on every token. You want the capacity of a large model with the inference cost of a small one. How do you decouple total parameters from per-token compute?

The contribution

Mixtral 8x7B is a Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) decoder. Each transformer layer replaces the single feed-forward block with 8 expert FFNs; a lightweight router picks the top-2 experts per token and combines their outputs by gating weight. The result: 47B total parameters but only ~13B active per token. Mixtral matches or beats Llama 2 70B and GPT-3.5 at far lower inference cost, again under Apache 2.0.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

Mixtral made open MoE practical and forced a mental split between two numbers that engineers must now track separately: total parameters (which dictate VRAM/storage — all experts must be resident) and active parameters (which dictate FLOPs/latency). MoE serving has its own headaches — expert load balancing, all-to-all communication — but the cost/quality trade is compelling.

The takeaway

"How big is the model?" is now two questions. Mixtral's 47B-total / 13B-active split means you size GPU memory for the total but estimate throughput from the active count — a distinction that carries straight into DeepSeek-V3.

DeepSeek-V3 — MLA, Auxiliary-Loss-Free MoE & FP8

DeepSeek-AI · 2024 · arXiv:2412.19437

The problem

Scaling MoE to frontier quality exposes three pain points: (1) the KV cache becomes the memory bottleneck at long context; (2) the standard auxiliary load-balancing loss that keeps experts evenly used fights the language-modelling objective and hurts quality; and (3) training a 600B-class model is ruinously expensive in FP16/BF16.

The contribution

DeepSeek-V3 is a 671B-parameter MoE with ~37B active per token that reaches frontier-class quality on a famously low compute budget, with open weights. Four ideas do the heavy lifting:

Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA)

Compress keys and values into a low-rank latent vector that is cached instead of full K/V. Shrinks the KV cache dramatically — the enabler for cheap 128K context — while preserving attention quality.

Auxiliary-loss-free balancing

The DeepSeekMoE design (fine-grained + shared experts) uses a per-expert bias adjusted dynamically to balance load, with no auxiliary loss. Removes the quality penalty that load-balancing losses normally impose.

FP8 mixed-precision training

One of the first production-scale runs to train largely in FP8, with fine-grained quantisation and careful accumulation. Roughly halves memory and compute versus BF16 — central to the low budget.

Multi-token prediction (MTP)

An auxiliary objective that predicts several future tokens per step, densifying the training signal and improving quality. The extra heads also enable speculative-style decoding at inference.

Why it matters to a practising engineer

DeepSeek-V3 showed that frontier-class quality is achievable with open weights and a startup-scale budget, upending assumptions about who can train top-tier models. MLA is now a sought-after KV-cache optimisation, and the auxiliary-loss-free balancing and FP8 recipe are widely cited templates for anyone building large MoE systems.

The takeaway

The frontier is no longer closed. MLA shrinks the KV cache, FP8 shrinks the training bill, and auxiliary-loss-free MoE removes the balancing tax — together they put a 671B open model within reach of a far smaller compute budget than anyone expected.

06

What to Take Away

  • The open-weights lineage is real. LLaMA → Llama 2 → Mistral → Mixtral → DeepSeek-V3 is a direct chain of borrowed and improved ideas — the RMSNorm + RoPE + SwiGLU template from LLaMA still underpins all of them.
  • Tokens beat parameters. LLaMA-13B matching GPT-3 175B proved that training data and efficiency, not raw size, define the quality/cost frontier.
  • Alignment is a pipeline. Llama 2's SFT → reward-model → RLHF (rejection sampling + PPO) recipe, plus GQA and Ghost Attention, made open chat models both shippable and serviceable.
  • Attention is where efficiency lives. GQA (shared KV heads), Mistral's sliding-window attention + rolling KV cache, and DeepSeek's MLA latent cache are all attacks on the same enemy: KV-cache cost at long context.
  • Total ≠ active parameters. Sparse MoE (Mixtral top-2 of 8; DeepSeek-V3 671B/37B) decouples capacity from per-token compute — size VRAM for total, estimate latency from active.
  • The frontier opened up. DeepSeek-V3's MLA + FP8 + auxiliary-loss-free MoE put frontier quality within a startup-scale budget, with open weights.
